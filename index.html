In simple terms: a ranking based only on what actually happened between the lines — who beat whom, nothing else.

The Colley Matrix ranks teams from wins and losses alone — a real network of who's beaten whom, based only on the final score, nothing else. It never factors in injuries, a team's reputation, or what anyone expected going in — only what actually happened on the field. Margin doesn't matter either; a 1-point win counts exactly the same as a 40-point blowout. Strength of schedule isn't guessed at or assigned by anyone — it emerges purely from how each team's real results connect to everyone else's, and that network only gets stronger as more games are actually played. Early in a season, with few connections between teams, it can disagree with the human poll a lot — that's the method being honest about how little evidence it has yet, not a flaw to explain away. Unlike the AP poll, it isn't tied to a weekly human vote — it updates as soon as any game actually finishes, even mid-week, so it can shift before the poll itself does.

In simple terms: a rough estimate of how safe a team's playoff spot looks, not a real simulation.

CFP Odds here are a simple, honest estimate, not a model that simulates the rest of the season. It starts from where a team sits relative to the 12-team field — the bye line at #4, the cutoff at #12, and the bubble beyond that — then nudges the number down for every loss already on the record. It doesn't know strength of schedule, who's left on the schedule, or tiebreakers, and it isn't trying to. Treat it as a quick gut-check next to the rankings, not a forecast to bet on.
